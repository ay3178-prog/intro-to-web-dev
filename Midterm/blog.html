01 · Hindi

The Language That Sounds Like Home

The one I grew up with

Hindi is the language of my family. It reminds me of the long phone calls in the car, the jokes at the dinner table, and the songs we would dance to at parties. It felt almost like second nature, something built into me that let me understand it long before I ever spoke it.

For a long time I answered in English when people spoke to me in Hindi, because I was scared of saying something wrong. I put an enormous amount of pressure on myself to perform. When I finally began trusting myself, my relationships with other people, with myself, and with the language changed. People got warmer, and the jokes started including me.

Dheere dheereslowly, slowly

Speaking Hindi taught me that a language is more than words. It's how you show the people you love that you're listening.

02 · Spanish

Spanish in a Crowd

The one I practice everywhere

Spanish is the language I hear all around me, on the train, in stores, and in crowds when I travel. Once I started learning it, I realized how much of the world I had been tuning out. Whether I was traveling for chess or helping someone with directions in an airport, Spanish surrounded me everywhere.

My biggest fears were talking too slowly, mixing up the languages I was learning, and making other people wait. But every time I spoke, it made people happy. They were glad to spend the time if it meant they got to speak their native language for a few minutes.

¿Cómo se dice...?how do you say...?

That one question has started more conversations for me than anything else, and it has let me connect with people across the world. It shows humility and a real desire to learn!

03 · Turkish

Learning Turkish by Ear

The one I learned by listening

I didn't learn Turkish from a textbook or by talking to a person. I learned it by listening, to songs, shows, and people talking, until the sounds started to turn into words I recognized. I started noticing patterns and connecting words, even though it felt scary at first.

Taking the leap to speaking in person was hard, and as always, I could understand much more than I could say. But I took that leap. I talked to the owners of an ice cream shop, went to dinner with a Turkish woman I met on the streets of New York, and had a conversation about chess in Turkish with a girl who stopped me because of my chess bag. Each story is a culmination of my hard work and care.

Kolay gelsinmay it come easy to you

It's something people say to someone who is working hard. It's also how I think about learning any language :)

04 · Italian (still learning)

Starting From Zero Again

The one I'm learning right now

Italian is the language I'm learning right now, which means I'm a beginner again. After speaking four languages, it's humbling to struggle with simple sentences.

Being a beginner again reminds me how it feels to be nervous, to mix up words, and to laugh at myself. Spanish helps a lot, but the way we call people who speak two languages "bilingual," I feel like I'm going "bye-lingual" with the words I already know.

Piano piano si va lontanoslowly, slowly, you go far

I'm writing this one as I go, so this story is still being written. It will take time to speak the language, but that's the exciting part.
