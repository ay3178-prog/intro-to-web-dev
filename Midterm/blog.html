01 · Hindi

The Language That Sounds Like Home

The one I grew up with

Hindi is the language of my family. It's in the long phone calls, the jokes at the dinner table, and the songs that play in the car. I understood it long before I felt brave enough to really speak it.

For a long time I answered in English when people spoke to me in Hindi, because I was scared of saying something wrong. The day I finally answered back in Hindi, the conversation changed. People got warmer, and the jokes started including me.

Dheere dheere (slowly, slowly)

Speaking Hindi taught me that a language is more than words. It's how you show the people you love that you're listening.

02 · Spanish

Spanish in a Crowd

The one I practice everywhere

Spanish is the language I hear all around me, on the train, in stores, and in crowds when I travel. Once I started learning it, I realized how much of the world I had been tuning out.

My biggest fear was talking too slowly and making people wait. What surprised me was how patient people were. Most of them were just happy I was trying.

¿Cómo se dice...? (How do you say...?)

That one question has started more conversations for me than anything else I know how to say.

03 · Turkish

Learning Turkish by Ear

The one I learned by listening

I didn't learn Turkish from a textbook. I learned it by listening, to songs, shows, and people talking, until the sounds started to turn into words I recognized.

The hardest part was that I could understand more than I could say. It felt like having a door half open. Speaking out loud, even with mistakes, is what finally pushed it open.

Kolay gelsin (may it come easy to you)

It's something people say to someone who is working hard. It's also how I think about learning any language.

04 · Italian (still learning)

Starting From Zero Again

The one I'm learning right now

Italian is the language I'm learning right now, which means I'm a beginner again. After speaking four languages, it's humbling to struggle with simple sentences.

Being a beginner again reminds me how it feels to be nervous, to mix up words, and to laugh at myself. Spanish helps a lot, and sometimes it gets me into trouble when the words look the same but mean something different.

Piano piano si va lontano (slowly, slowly, you go far)

I'm writing this one as I go, so this story is still being written.
